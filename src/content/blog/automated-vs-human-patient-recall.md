## Introduction
In today's healthcare landscape, practices face the challenge of reactivating dormant patients efficiently. This leads to the question: **Automated vs Human Patient Recall-what works better?** In this article, we will explore both methods, their advantages, and provide actionable insights for healthcare providers.

## Understanding Patient Recall
Patient recall refers to strategies aimed at bringing back patients who have not visited a practice for an extended period. Reactivating these patients can significantly impact revenue, making it crucial to understand the methods available. For more on this topic, check out our article on [dormant patient reactivation](https://mybcat.com/blog/dormant-patient-reactivation/).

## The Case for Automation
Automation in patient recall utilizes technology to streamline processes. Here are some key advantages:
- **Cost-Effectiveness**: Automated systems often require less financial investment compared to hiring human staff.
- **Efficiency**: Automated reminders can reach a larger number of patients simultaneously through various channels such as email or SMS.
- **24/7 Availability**: Automated systems can operate around the clock, ensuring that patients receive reminders at any time.

### Drawbacks of Automation
While automation has its advantages, there are notable downsides:
- **Lack of Personal Touch**: Automated messages can feel impersonal and may not resonate with patients who prefer human interaction.
- **Limited Customization**: Automated systems may not address specific patient needs or concerns, potentially leading to missed opportunities for reactivation.

## The Human Touch in Patient Recall
Human patient recall involves employing staff to reach out personally to patients. Here’s why this can be effective:
- **Personalization**: Human agents can tailor conversations to individual patient needs, fostering a connection that automated systems cannot replicate.
- **Trust Building**: Patients are more likely to respond positively to a familiar voice rather than a robotic message. This can enhance patient loyalty and satisfaction.

### Drawbacks of Human Recall
However, relying solely on human agents has its challenges:
- **Higher Costs**: Employing staff for patient recall can be more expensive than using automated systems.
- **Scalability Issues**: As practices grow, scaling human recall efforts can be difficult without additional hiring and training.

## Hybrid Approaches: Combining the Best of Both Worlds
Many practices are finding success by integrating automated systems with human agents. This hybrid approach allows for:
- **Efficiency with Personalization**: Initial outreach can be automated to handle large volumes, followed by personalized follow-ups from staff.
- **Data-Driven Insights**: Automated systems can track engagement metrics, providing human agents with valuable information to tailor their approach.

For more insights on how to effectively manage patient intake, read our [centralized vs distributed intake framework](https://mybcat.com/blog/centralized-vs-distributed-intake-framework/).

## Practical Steps for Implementation
Here’s a checklist for practices looking to implement or enhance their patient recall systems:
1. **Assess Current Systems**: Evaluate the effectiveness of existing recall methods.
2. **Determine Budget**: Consider your budget for automation vs human resources.
3. **Choose Tools**: Select software solutions that integrate well with your practice management system.
4. **Train Staff**: Ensure that human agents are well-trained in communication techniques and patient engagement.
5. **Monitor Results**: Track the effectiveness of your recall efforts and adjust strategies as needed.

## Conclusion
Both automated and human patient recall systems have their strengths and weaknesses. The best approach often lies in combining both to maximize patient engagement and revenue.
