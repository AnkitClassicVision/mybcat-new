An optometry practice sale letter of intent, or LOI, outlines the proposed transaction before the parties complete their definitive agreements. It brings purchase price, timing, conditions, and expectations into a document both sides can review.

Some provisions, including confidentiality or exclusivity, may be binding even when the principal business terms are not. [Dental & Medical Counsel](https://www.dmcounsel.com/blog/10-key-terms-for-an-optometry-practice-letter-of-intent).

For an owner, the central question is whether the written proposal reflects the transition you actually want. An attractive price deserves careful consideration alongside the assets included, payment conditions, work you would continue doing, and obligations you would accept.

Whether you own a solo practice or several offices, approach the LOI as a connected set of decisions. Read it with your transaction attorney, identify unresolved questions, and make your priorities explicit before signing.

## What an Optometry Practice Sale Letter of Intent Establishes

### A written outline of the proposed agreement

The American Dental Association describes an LOI as a “roadmap for the entire process.” Its guidance lists purchase price, assets, financing, seller employment, and lease arrangements among the subjects to address.

Although written for dental practices, these categories provide a useful comparison for optometry sellers. [American Dental Association](https://www.ada.org/resources/careers/career-planning/what-makes-a-successful-sale).

Use that outline to check whether you and the buyer understand the proposal in the same way. If you expect to leave clinical work shortly after closing, identify any language suggesting an ongoing employment commitment.

If you intend to retain particular equipment or real estate, make those expectations visible.

Mark unresolved matters explicitly. A topic described as subject to further agreement should remain on your decision list until you understand the proposed resolution.

### A framework suited to the actual buyer

The transaction deserves attention on its own terms. Optometry attorney Craig Steinberg distinguishes sales between optometrists from private equity acquisitions and identifies the LOI as a place to address whether the transaction involves an “asset sale or a stock sale.” [Craig S.

Steinberg, O.D., J.D.](https://www.csteinberglaw.org/od-od-practice-sales).

Start by confirming who would buy the practice, what business or locations the proposal covers, and what ownership interest or assets would change hands. For a multi-office practice, ask for clear treatment of each location.

If an associate is the prospective buyer, give the written proposal the same careful attention you would give an outside offer. Use your existing familiarity to discuss expectations openly, while documenting the decisions that matter to both parties.

## Define the Transaction and Its Economics

### Identify included and excluded assets

Review the description of the sale before focusing exclusively on price. An attorney writing for Review of Optometric Business includes price, included and excluded assets, liabilities, holdbacks, and restrictive covenants among the LOI terms to examine.

[Review of Optometric Business](https://staging.reviewob.com/7-due-diligence-steps-to-protect-yourself-before-buying-a-practice).

Prepare a working list of the equipment, optical inventory, business name, and other items you expect the transaction to include.

Add a separate list of anything you intend to retain. Ask your attorney which details belong in the LOI and which should be developed in the definitive documents.

For each item, ask whether the wording reflects your understanding. Replace broad assumptions about what comes with the practice with a description your advisers can evaluate.

### Read price alongside payment conditions

Write down the proposed purchase price, when payment would occur, and any conditions attached to payment. If a holdback appears, ask what it covers, what would permit its release, and which terms remain unresolved.

Keep these questions connected during negotiation. When a buyer revises the price, review the rest of the proposal again rather than treating the revised figure as the entire offer.

Ask your accountant to evaluate the proposed economics and your attorney to explain the obligations. The American Optometric Association identifies “practice appraisal, tax considerations and contracts” as areas for appropriate professional assistance during a sale.

[American Optometric Association](https://www.aoa.org/AOA/Documents/Education/Education_PDFs/Case%20Study%2016-Selling%20a%20Practice.pdf).

### Address receivables and existing obligations

Eyes On Eyecare’s acquisition checklist includes accounts receivable, practice debt, financing, lease terms, and expectations after closing.

These are useful subjects for a seller’s review even though the article addresses buyers. [Eyes On Eyecare](https://eyesoneyecare.com/resources/7-key-steps-purchasing-optometry-practice).

Ask how outstanding receivables would be handled and who would perform any related collection work. Request an explanation of which obligations the buyer proposes to assume and which would remain with you.

Use a simple discussion worksheet:

| Topic | Question to resolve |
|---|---|
| Receivables | Who receives collections associated with work performed before closing? |
| Collection responsibilities | Who handles the work and reporting? |
| Equipment obligations | What happens to existing equipment leases or balances? |
| Vendor obligations | Which commitments would continue, transfer, or end? |
| Open issues | Who will investigate each unresolved item? |

Have your advisers translate the agreed treatment into appropriate transaction language.

## Understand Which Commitments May Already Be Binding

### Review binding language clause by clause

Avoid relying on a general description of the LOI as nonbinding. Dental & Medical Counsel specifically identifies “confidentiality and exclusivity” as provisions that may carry binding legal weight even when principal business terms do not.

[Dental & Medical Counsel](https://www.dmcounsel.com/blog/10-key-terms-for-an-optometry-practice-letter-of-intent).

Ask your attorney to identify the provisions that could create obligations upon signing. For each, request a plain-language explanation of what you must do, what you must avoid, and how the obligation ends.

Give proposed restrictive covenants separate attention. Describe any future clinical work, teaching, consulting, or ownership activities you hope to pursue, and ask counsel to evaluate the proposed language against those plans.

### Define the boundaries of exclusivity

Treat exclusivity as a decision about your sale process. Ask what discussions or activities the proposed clause would restrict, when it would begin, and how it would end.

A physician practice sale checklist from the American College of Foot and Ankle Surgeons discusses no-shop agreements that restrict discussions with other potential buyers during a defined period.

This is a healthcare comparison for discussion with counsel, rather than an optometry-specific rule. [ACFAS](https://www.acfas.org/getattachment/4398eb6d-060c-44a2-9d92-8ba75baa2f1c/Checklist-for-Physicians-Selling-a-Practice-2019.pdf).

Before agreeing, discuss the work expected during that period. Ask how the parties would handle an extension request, delayed information, or unresolved financing. Have counsel explain the actual wording rather than assuming that silence or inactivity ends the obligation.

### Establish confidentiality and communication expectations

The same ACFAS checklist discusses confidentiality agreements and the LOI’s role in recording preliminary terms. [ACFAS](https://www.acfas.org/getattachment/4398eb6d-060c-44a2-9d92-8ba75baa2f1c/Checklist-for-Physicians-Selling-a-Practice-2019.pdf).

Use the negotiation to establish a deliberate communication process. Identify who will coordinate information requests, who may speak for each side, and how questions about a possible sale should be handled.

Keep preliminary discussion at the business-summary level, and have counsel direct the handling of sensitive information. Separately, agree on how proposed announcements will be reviewed.

Staff communication, patient communication, and exchanges with advisers deserve clear ownership rather than assumptions about who will act.

## Set a Workable Path Through Diligence and Closing

### Assign responsibilities for the review process

An ADA transition case describes a sale that unraveled amid unclear responsibilities, financing documentation issues, and later disagreements. Its discussion emphasizes defining terms and timing in the LOI and involving an attorney familiar with practice transitions.

The case concerns dentistry, but the questions it raises are relevant to organizing an optometry sale. [American Dental Association](https://www.ada.org/resources/careers/career-planning/articles/what-went-wrong-my-transition-fell-through-after-months-of-planning).

Apply that lesson by requesting a written list of expected diligence materials and assigning a coordinator on each side. Identify which documents are available, which need preparation, and which require adviser review before release.

Maintain a shared list of unresolved business questions without including patient details. Record the question, the person responsible for answering it, and the decision needed. Discuss material findings with your advisers before agreeing to revised terms.

### Connect financing, the lease, and the closing date

Visionary Practice Group identifies financing, due diligence, lease terms, receivables, and the seller’s continuing role among the subjects addressed during sale negotiations.

Its process continues through diligence and closing. [Visionary Practice Group](https://visionarypracticegroup.com/sell-optometry-practice).

Review the proposed closing date alongside those dependencies. Ask what financing condition the buyer is requesting and what remains to be resolved about the premises.

If you own the building, clarify whether the proposal includes its sale, a lease arrangement, or a separate discussion.

For each open matter, identify the next action and the person responsible. Ask counsel how the documents would address an unmet condition or proposed extension.

Choose timing through that discussion. Avoid inserting a familiar-looking deadline without first checking whether the required work can be organized around it.

## Describe Your Role and the Practice Handoff

### Make the seller’s work commitment explicit

A buyer may address continued employment directly in its LOI. MyEyeDr.’s published process says its LOI details purchase price, “your employment commitment,” and other conditions, followed by diligence and legal agreement review.

This describes that buyer’s process; it does not establish terms for every optometry sale. [MyEyeDr.](https://acquisitions.myeyedr.com/optometry-partnership-opportunities/process).

Write down the role you would consider accepting. Include the type of work, expected schedule, location, duration, and decision-making responsibilities you want discussed. Ask which points the buyer is prepared to address in the LOI and which require an employment agreement.

Review any request for transition assistance with the same care. Specify the tasks you would be willing to perform, such as introductions or explaining established workflows. Bring uncertainties back to the negotiation before treating the role as settled.

### Plan patient and staff communication deliberately

The AOA’s discussion of practice sales addresses patient notification and continuity of care, including transitions to an outside buyer or an existing associate.

[American Optometric Association](https://www.aoa.org/AOA/Documents/Education/Education_PDFs/Case%20Study%2016-Selling%20a%20Practice.pdf).

Use the LOI discussion to establish responsibility for developing the handoff plan. Ask who will coordinate patient communication, who will answer staff questions, and how you will review proposed messages with the buyer.

Then identify practical topics for the later operating plan: appointment handling, unfinished administrative work, questions arriving through the front desk, and the seller’s availability during transition. Keep patient-specific information out of the LOI.

For several offices, discuss whether responsibilities differ by location. For a solo practice, identify the work you personally handle that someone else will need to understand. Ask for named responsibilities and a process for resolving questions.

## Review the Whole Proposal Before Signing

### Compare the document with your own priorities

Prepare a short owner’s brief before your review meeting. Describe your preferred exit, the work you would continue doing, the assets you intend to retain, and the matters that require further advice.

Use that brief to read the LOI from beginning to end. Mark each significant topic as acceptable, needing revision, or unresolved. Explain why a revision matters so your advisers can understand the underlying objective.

Consider the proposal as a whole. If you are willing to change your departure timing, identify what arrangement you would accept.

If keeping the building matters to you, make that preference part of the real estate discussion. Give your team specific instructions rather than asking them simply to obtain better terms.

### Carry agreed terms into the next stage

Before signing, ask your attorney to walk through the current version and identify the remaining decisions. Confirm that the draft reflects the negotiated changes and that you understand any obligations that begin immediately.

After signing, keep the LOI available as a reference during review of the definitive agreements. Ask your advisers to flag differences in price, included assets, conditions, employment expectations, and other material terms. Review each proposed change deliberately.

Continue using a clear question list throughout diligence. When an issue is resolved, record the decision and have the appropriate adviser address it in the documents. Your aim is a proposal you understand and a transition you are prepared to carry out.
