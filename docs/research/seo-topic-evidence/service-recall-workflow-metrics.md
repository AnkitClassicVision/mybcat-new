## Evidence set

1. [Are Appointment Reminders Allowed Under HIPAA?](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/286/are-appointment-reminders-allowed-under-hipaa-without-authorization/index.html)
   - Publisher: U.S. Department of Health and Human Services
   - Accessed: 2026-08-11
   - Bounded evidence: HHS states that appointment reminders are part of treatment and may be made without a separate authorization under the HIPAA Privacy Rule.
   - Supports: reminders can be part of care operations, subject to privacy safeguards
2. [May Providers Leave Messages for Patients?](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/198/may-health-care-providers-leave-messages/index.html)
   - Publisher: U.S. Department of Health and Human Services
   - Accessed: 2026-08-11
   - Bounded evidence: HHS permits patient communications by phone or mail but advises limiting message content, using professional judgment, and honoring reasonable confidential-communication requests.
   - Supports: contact workflows need minimum-necessary message rules and preference handling
3. [Patient Portal Reminder After No-Show in Ophthalmology](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11162931/)
   - Publisher: PubMed Central
   - Accessed: 2026-08-11
   - Bounded evidence: A randomized ophthalmology trial found that a portal message after a missed visit was associated with greater reattendance within the study's follow-up window than a mailed letter alone.
   - Supports: recall measurement can include post-no-show reengagement by channel
4. [Outpatient Appointment Reminder Systems and No-Shows](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0002934310001087)
   - Publisher: The American Journal of Medicine
   - Accessed: 2026-08-11
   - Bounded evidence: A randomized multispecialty outpatient study compared staff calls, automated reminders, and no reminders, finding different attendance and cancellation outcomes by reminder method.
   - Supports: recall reporting should separate outreach method and disposition
5. [Telephone Reminders and Kept Appointments](https://www.jabfm.org/content/14/3/193)
   - Publisher: Journal of the American Board of Family Medicine
   - Accessed: 2026-08-11
   - Bounded evidence: A randomized outpatient trial found that reminder calls changed both no-show and cancellation behavior, allowing released slots to be reused in that setting.
   - Supports: recall workflow metrics should include cancellations and recovered capacity
